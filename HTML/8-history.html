History of Music

Discover the range of music that have existed way back in the 1700s or 1800s. The history of formal Western art music gained significant definition during the Classical period, spanning roughly from 1750 to 1820. During the Classical period, composers standardized instrumental arrangements like symphonies, concertos, and string quartets, emphasizing structural clarity and balance over the complex polyphony of the earlier Baroque era.

Classical Era

Prominent figures from the First Viennese School, including Joseph Haydn and Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, established the foundations of classical composition. Ludwig van Beethoven subsequently expanded the structural boundaries of symphonies and sonatas, introducing intense emotional depth and aiding the transition into the nineteenth-century Romantic era alongside other influential composers like Franz Schubert and Frédéric Chopin.

Romantic Era and Modernism

The Romantic era prioritised emotional expression and dramatic narratives, giving rise to large-scale orchestral works and solo piano masterpieces. Later, the twentieth century brought radical experimentation with the emergence of Modernism, Impressionism, and Expressionism, led by composers such as Claude Debussy and Igor Stravinsky, breaking away from traditional tonal systems.

Technological advancements in the mid-to-late twentieth century transformed the dissemination of music through the mass production of vinyl records and radio broadcasting. The shift from physical sheet music to recorded media allowed diverse genres, including jazz, rock, and pop music, to reach global audiences and establish the foundations of the modern commercial music industry.
